1. What the app does

The app is a personal health and fitness diary. It counts steps, records GPS workouts, guides exercises, yoga and breathing, and lets you log food, water, sleep, weight, blood pressure, blood glucose, heart rate, SpO₂, lab results, medicines, periods and readings for family members. It shows goals, scores, reminders and summaries based on what you record.

2. Not medical advice

Nalam is a wellness app, not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease. Scores, insights and messages (for example readiness, sleep score, estimated HbA1c, time in range, BMI, heart-rate zones, cycle and PCOS-pattern insights, and lab "normal ranges") are general information only. Always follow your doctor's advice on diagnosis, medicines, insulin, diet and exercise, and never stop or change a treatment because of something the app shows. Before starting a new exercise, yoga or fasting routine, check with your doctor if you are pregnant, have a heart condition, diabetes, high blood pressure or any other health condition.

3. Accuracy of measurements

Step counts, distance, calories, floors, sleep detection and GPS routes are estimates from your phone's sensors and can be wrong. Heart rate, stress and HRV measured with the camera, and readings from Bluetooth devices, depend on the device and how it is used, and are not medical-grade. Food nutrition values are approximate; barcode values come from Open Food Facts, a public database that may contain errors.

4. Safety features: SOS and fall detection

SOS and fall detection are an extra aid, not a replacement for emergency services. Fall detection can miss real falls and can trigger when you have not fallen. Sending an SOS depends on your phone being on, having signal and SMS credit, the permissions being allowed and your emergency contact being correct, and your location may be unavailable or inaccurate. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (112 in India).

5. Reminders and alarms

Medicine reminders, alarms and other notifications depend on your phone allowing them. Battery savers, "Do not disturb" and similar settings can delay or block them. Do not rely on the app alone for medicines or time-critical events.

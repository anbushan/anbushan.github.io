1. What the app does

The app scans the Wi-Fi network you are connected to and lists its devices, alerts you to new or unknown devices, tests internet speed, keeps speed and outage history, and makes a provider report card and complaint message from your results. It also offers a router security check, hidden camera check, Bluetooth tracker scan, on-device app firewall, data use per app with data plan alerts, a Wi-Fi analyzer, signal meter, free Wi-Fi finder, mobile network information, and network tools such as ping, traceroute, DNS, whois, public IP, IP calculator, Wake-on-LAN, remote shutdown over SSH, iPerf3 and phone-to-phone speed tests, a worldwide check and a website monitor.

2. Use it on networks you are allowed to use

Scan, port-scan, test and connect only to networks, devices and computers that you own or have permission to check. Join open Wi-Fi networks only where you are allowed to use them; using someone else's network without permission can be illegal. The app does not reveal or guess Wi-Fi passwords and must not be used to gain access you are not entitled to. You are responsible for the commands you send with remote shutdown and for the sites you monitor.

3. Accuracy and limits

Results depend on your phone, Android version, network and the devices on it. Android 10 and later hides most devices' MAC addresses, so makers and names can be missing or guessed. Device types, the network health score, channel advice and the router security check are estimates from what the app can see from inside your network; they are not a full security audit. The hidden camera check finds only cameras connected to the same Wi-Fi, and the tracker scan finds only trackers that are advertising nearby. Speed tests measure your connection to the test server at that moment. Data use comes from Android's counters and may differ from your mobile operator's figures.

4. Provider report card and complaints

The report card summarises the speed tests stored on your phone and compares them with the plan speed you enter. It is information to help you talk to your provider, not a certified measurement. You decide whether to send the complaint message, and you are responsible for what it says.

5. App firewall

The firewall uses Android's VPN feature on your phone. Android allows only one VPN at a time, so it turns off if another VPN app starts. Some system traffic may not be blocked. Blocking apps can stop them from working.

6. Your data

Your device lists, history and settings are stored only on your phone. They are deleted if you uninstall the app or clear its storage, and we cannot recover them, so use Settings → Backup if you want to keep them. See the Privacy Policy.

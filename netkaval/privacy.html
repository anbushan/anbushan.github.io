Privacy Policy

Last updated: 21 November 2026

NetKaval ("the app", "we") is an Android app for checking your Wi-Fi and internet connection, developed by Anbushan. This policy explains what the app does with your information. In short: the app has no accounts or sign-in, network scans and checks run on your phone, and what the app learns (device lists, history, settings) is stored only on your phone. We do not run servers that receive your data. Some tools contact outside services to do their job, and the app uses Google AdMob for ads, as described in section 4.

1. Information stored on your phone

  • Your networks and devices. When you scan, the app finds devices on the Wi-Fi network you are connected to and saves their IP address, MAC address (where Android allows it), maker, name, type and open ports, plus the names you give them, whether you trust them, and when they were online. This lets the app alert you to new devices and show a 7-day online history.
  • Network name. The Wi-Fi name (SSID) and router identifier (BSSID) are saved so each network keeps its own device list.
  • Speed and outage history. Speed test results, internet outages, your plan speed and provider name (if you enter them for the report card), and alerts are saved on your phone.
  • Data use. If you allow usage access, the app reads Android's own per-app data counters to show which apps use the most data, to warn you about your data plan, and to notice when an app uses the internet for the first time. It keeps a list of app IDs it has already seen; the counters themselves are read from Android each time.
  • Tools and settings. Websites you monitor, firewall rules, data plan limits and other preferences are saved on your phone.
  • Backups and sharing. A backup file (device names, networks, speed history, monitored sites) is written only where you choose to save it. Speed result images, report cards and complaint messages are made on your phone and handed to the app you choose; from there that app's own privacy policy applies.

2. Location and nearby devices

Android only shows Wi-Fi network names, nearby Wi-Fi networks and mobile signal details to apps with location permission. NetKaval uses location permission only for this. It never reads your GPS position or coordinates, does not track you, and does not use location in the background beyond reading the Wi-Fi name during scheduled scans.

"Nearby devices" permission lets the app read Wi-Fi details and scan for Bluetooth devices and trackers (such as AirTags) near you. It is declared as not used for location. Bluetooth scan results are shown on screen and are not saved.

3. Firewall, usage access and other sensitive features

  • App firewall (VPN service). When you turn the firewall on, the app creates a local VPN on your phone. Only the apps you choose to block are routed into it, and their traffic is dropped on your phone. NetKaval does not read, log, store or send anyone's traffic, and traffic from other apps does not pass through it. It does not connect to any VPN server.
  • Usage access is optional and used only for the data-per-app, data plan and new-app features described above.
  • Remote shutdown (SSH). The username and password you type are sent only to the computer you choose, used once for that command and never saved.
  • Free Wi-Fi finder. When you tap Connect, the app asks Android to save that open network; Android asks you to confirm.

5. Permissions

  • Location – Wi-Fi name, nearby networks and mobile signal details (see section 2). Optional.
  • Nearby devices (Wi-Fi and Bluetooth) – Wi-Fi details and the tracker scan. Optional.
  • Notifications (Android 13+) – new-device, outage, website-monitor, data plan and daily summary alerts. Optional.
  • Usage access – data per app, data plan and new-app alerts. Optional, granted in Android settings.
  • VPN – the app firewall, only when you turn it on.
  • Foreground service – the live speed meter in the status bar, only when you turn it on.
  • Wi-Fi state and multicast – scanning your network, discovering devices and offering open networks to Android.
  • Internet and advertising ID – ads, speed tests and online tools.
  • Start at boot – restarting the live speed meter and scheduled checks after a restart.

The app can open Android's battery settings so you can let it run in the background (no extra permission). It shows which launchable apps are installed only to label data use and the firewall list. It does not request access to your contacts, camera, microphone, photos or call log.

6. Your choices and deleting data

  • Remove any permission at any time in Android settings, and turn each alert or background check off in the app.
  • Delete single devices, speed history or monitored sites in the app, or everything under Settings → Clear all data.
  • Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android settings, deletes everything it stored on your phone. Backup files and things you shared stay wherever you saved or sent them.

7. Children

The app is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect information from children.
